## 🔴 TIER 0 — must do ASAP (this week)

### 1. Write your About page

**This is the single most urgent thing on the list**, because it's not just
missing — it's *visibly* missing. Anyone who clicks "about" on your live site
right now sees grey boxes labelled **"for Yūki to fill in."** That reads as an
abandoned site, which is worse than having no about page at all.

Four sections are waiting for you. Rough targets:

| section | length | what goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| **who i am** | 3–4 paragraphs | How you found this work and when. What you did before. What made you start doing it for other people. Who you love working with most. |
| **training & certifications** | a list | Each one: what it is, who it was through, the year. Reiki levels and lineage, death doula training, any CBT or coaching programme, anything else. |
| **how i work** | 2–3 paragraphs | What someone should expect. What a session actually looks like from their side. This is the part that makes people decide you're the right person. |
| **what i don't do** | short | Your actual limits. Medical advice, diagnoses, promises about outcomes, contacting people who haven't consented. Naming your boundaries makes people trust you *more*, not less. |

**Write it in your own voice** — the same voice as your service descriptions.
Lowercase, direct, a bit funny. Don't switch into "professional website" mode;
the whole site is built around how you actually talk.

Nothing on that page was written for you on purpose. Claims about your training
should only ever come from you.

### 3. One good photo of your face

Not a professional shoot. One photo, good enough to use everywhere.

There is currently **not a single image of you** on your website, and that is
the biggest thing costing you bookings. People buying something this personal
want to see who they're trusting. A face converts better than any amount of
copy.

**How to get one in 20 minutes with a phone:**
- Outdoors, in shade, or just inside a window. Never direct sun, never overhead
  indoor lighting.
- Phone at eye height, not below. Have someone else take it, or use a timer —
  arm's-length selfies read as casual.
- Portrait mode if you have it. Take 40 and pick 3.
- Look at the lens, not the screen.
- Wear what you'd actually wear to work.

Send it over and it goes on the site.

### 5. A short intro video

**60 to 90 seconds. Filmed on your phone. Not scripted, not edited.**

This is the highest-trust thing you can make. For distance work especially,
people want to know there's a real person on the other end.

What to say, roughly:
1. Who you are and what you do (10s)
2. What a distance session actually involves — because most people have no
   idea (30s)
3. Who it helps most (20s)
4. How to book (10s)

Film it once, badly, and post it. A polished video you never make is worth
nothing. This goes on the site, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok.

Two quotes are sitting in your admin as drafts because they came from private

### 7. Start asking every single client for a review

You have six testimonials. You should have sixty. The only reason you don't is
that nobody's asking.

**Send this the day after a session, while they still feel it:**

> hey — really glad we got to work together. if you have two minutes, would you
> mind leaving a review on my Facebook page? it genuinely helps people find me.
> no pressure at all if not 🖤

Roughly one in three will do it. That's the whole trick — there isn't a clever
version.

Put every review you get into the admin so it shows on the site too.

## 🟡 TIER 2 — growth (next 1–3 months)

### 8. Lean harder on Facebook — it's already working

Your best marketing right now is Diana posting *"go book her"* to her friends.
That's worth more than any ad, and it's free.

- Post 2–3 times a week. Consistency beats quality.
- Share every new testimonial as its own post

### 9. Instagram + TikTok

Same content, minimal extra work. Reiki and spiritual work do genuinely well on

Things that perform:

Post the same thing to both. Don't overthink it.

### 11. Google Business Profile — worth trying, but don't lose a week to it

**Read this before you start, because the usual advice doesn't fit you.**

Everyone will tell you to set up a Google Business Profile. For most local
businesses that's right. For you it's genuinely marginal, for two reasons:

**Verification is now a real obstacle.** Video verification has replaced
postcards as the default, and for a business with no storefront Google expects
you to demonstrate legitimacy through things like branded vehicles, equipment
and signage — which is designed around plumbers and landscapers, not someone
doing energy work from home. You'd also need a real physical address on file
(no PO boxes, no virtual offices), plus business registration documents or an
EIN letter.

**And the payoff is smaller for you than for a normal local business.** Google
Business Profile's whole strength is "near me" searches. Your work is *distance*
reiki — your clients aren't geographically bound, so the thing it's best at is
the thing you need least.

**My honest advice:** try it once, give it an hour, and if verification stalls,
drop it and put that hour into Tier 1 instead. It is not the unlock people will
tell you it is. If you do go for it, use your real address (it stays hidden from
the public), set a service area, and pick your primary category carefully —
choosing the wrong business type at signup is a common cause of suspension.

Sources if you want to dig in: [Google's own SAB
guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en),
[eligibility
rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en), and a
[practical write-up of the 2026 verification
flow](https://searchengineland.com/google-get-service-area-business-verified-461330).

## Things you should NOT do

- **Don't publish testimonials that claim you cured a medical condition.**
  Two of yours were trimmed for exactly this reason. Claims about reversing
  injuries or curing anxiety are the specific thing regulators act on, and the
  risk lands on you, not the person who wrote it.
- **Don't pay for ads yet.** Not until the about page, photos and a video are
  done. Ads sending people to a half-finished site is money set on fire.
- **Don't buy a fancy website redesign.** You have one. Fill it in.
- **Don't promise outcomes** in any copy you write. "What people have
  experienced" is safe; "this will fix your anxiety" is not.
